Ranthambore Safari Shifts, Timings & Seasonal Schedule

Ranthambore National Park offers exciting wildlife safari experiences in two daily shifts – morning safari and afternoon safari. Each drive provides visitors with an opportunity to explore the forest, observe wildlife and experience the natural beauty of the park.

Morning safari usually begins around sunrise, while the afternoon safari takes place later in the day and ends before sunset. Exact reporting and departure timings vary throughout the year depending on seasonal daylight hours and official forest department guidelines.

Ranthambore's regular safari season generally operates from October to June. During the monsoon months of July, August and September, safari access is generally limited to selected zones, including Zones 6 to 10, subject to official park regulations and availability.

Visitors planning a Ranthambore Tiger Safari are advised to check the latest shift timings, zone availability and booking details before their visit. Please arrive at the designated boarding point according to the reporting time mentioned on your confirmed safari permit.

Morning safari

Cooler hours, when the forest becomes active after sunrise. Visitors may see deer, birds, monkeys, reptiles and, with luck, larger predators.

Afternoon safari

Starts later and runs toward sunset. The changing light gives the forest a different atmosphere and animals may become more active as temperatures fall.

Explore All 10 Ranthambore Safari Zones – Routes, Wildlife & Forest Experience

Ranthambore National Park is a famous wildlife park in the state of Rajasthan, known for its Bengal tigers. It was declared a national park in 1980 and is one of the best-known national parks in North India. The park takes its name from the old Ranthambore Fort, which stands right inside the park.

Many people travel here because it is one of the best places in India to see Bengal tigers in the wild. On a safari ride you get a chance to see tigers living freely in their natural home, which is the main reason tourists from India and all over the world visit. No sighting can be guaranteed, because the animals are wild and move freely.

Other Wildlife and Birds

Besides tigers, the park is home to many other animals. If you look closely during your visit, you may see leopards, sloth bears, hyenas, wild boars, sambar deer and chital.

Ranthambore is also a fantastic place for birdwatching, with around 300 kinds of resident and visiting birds. Some common birds you can spot are the Kingfisher, Painted Spurfowl, Sarus Crane, Large Cormorant, Bronze-winged Jacana, Sandpiper, Nightjar, Great Horned Owl and Painted Sandgrouse.

Forest, Lakes and Natural Landscape

The park is made up of thick green forests, open grassy fields, rocky hills, and many ponds and lakes. These natural habitats give animals and birds food, fresh water and safe places to live, so many types of wildlife can thrive inside the park.

The lakes and water holes are especially important for animals when the weather gets very hot. Visitors can enjoy these scenic spots while looking for wildlife on a safari.

Ranthambore Safari Zone 1 – Singh Dwar, Sultanpur & Tuti Ka Nalla

Ranthambore Safari Zone 1 is a popular core safari zone of Ranthambore National Park, Rajasthan, known for its rugged forest landscape, rocky hills, natural waterholes and diverse wildlife. The safari route starts from Singh Dwar Gate and takes visitors through different sections of the dry deciduous forest, offering an exciting jungle safari experience.

Zone 1 Landscape, Forest Trails and Safari Route

Unlike the relatively open landscapes around the lakes, Zone 1 features winding forest tracks, rocky slopes, narrow valleys and natural drainage channels known as nallas. Important areas along the safari route include Tala, Sultanpur, Amreshwar Dang, Gada Dub, Peela Pani and Tuti Ka Nalla.

These forest sections create opportunities to observe wildlife at relatively close distances. During the dry season, waterholes such as Peela Pani and Tuti Ka Nalla can attract animals, making them interesting locations for wildlife observation and photography. The safari route follows a loop and returns to the Singh Dwar entry gate.

Entry Gate and Distance from Ranthambore Hotels

Zone 1 safari vehicles enter through Singh Dwar Gate, located on the Sherpur side of Ranthambore National Park. The gate is accessible from many hotels and resorts along Ranthambore Road. The approximate driving time from the main hotel area is 15 to 25 minutes, depending on the property and traffic conditions.

Safari guests are generally picked up around one hour before their scheduled safari slot. This allows sufficient time for hotel pickups, reaching the entry gate and completing the required identification checks. Travellers staying in Sawai Madhopur town or towards Kundera may need additional travel time.

Tigers and Other Wildlife in Zone 1

Zone 1 has been associated with several well-known Bengal tigers over the years. Noor (T-39) became particularly associated with this forest area, where she raised multiple litters. The zone was also historically known for Ustad (T-24), who was relocated from Ranthambore in 2015.

Apart from Bengal tigers, visitors may encounter leopards, sambar deer, spotted deer (chital), nilgai, langurs and different bird species. Sloth bears and birds of prey may also be spotted around the rocky forest sections. Wildlife sightings depend on animal movement, weather, season and natural conditions. No tiger sighting is guaranteed during a safari.

Best Time to Visit Ranthambore Zone 1

The main safari season generally runs from October to June. Zone 1, being a core zone, normally remains closed during the monsoon period from 1 July to 30 September, subject to official forest department notifications.

October to February: Pleasant weather, greener forest surroundings and comfortable morning safari conditions make these months suitable for exploring the zone.

March to June: Temperatures rise during summer, and wildlife activity around available water sources can make this an interesting period for wildlife observation.

Both morning and afternoon safari slots offer different experiences. Morning drives provide pleasant temperatures and soft natural light, while afternoon safaris offer opportunities to explore the forest during the warmer hours.

Jeep Safari vs Canter Safari in Zone 1

Both Jeep and Canter safaris operate in Zone 1, subject to availability and permit allocation. The choice depends on your travel group, budget and safari preferences.

  • Jeep Safari: A six-seater open safari vehicle that is suitable for small groups, wildlife photographers and travellers who prefer a more personal safari experience.
  • Canter Safari: A larger open safari vehicle suitable for families and groups looking for a shared safari experience.

Both vehicle options follow the designated safari routes and timings. The forest department controls vehicle entry and movement within the park.

Please note that the final safari zone is allocated through the forest department's permit system. A specific zone cannot be guaranteed in advance. Zone 2 – Jogi Mahal, Jhalra & Phuta Bandha

Zone 2 offers a combination of forest vegetation, open spaces and natural water bodies. The safari route includes Jogi Mahal Gate, Jhalra, Kamaldhar, Amrai, Phuta Bandha, Pandudeh and Gandhria. These areas create an interesting setting for visitors interested in exploring the natural habitat of Ranthambore.

During a safari, visitors may encounter Bengal tigers, leopards, sambar deer, spotted deer, langurs and various resident and migratory birds. Water sources and forest clearings are important parts of the area's wildlife habitat.

Experience: A mix of woodland scenery, water points and wildlife observation.

Popular route areas: Jhalra, Kamaldhar, Amrai, Phuta Bandha and Jogi Mahal.

Zone 3 – Padam Talao, Raj Bagh Lake & Jogi Mahal

Ranthambore Safari Zone 3 is known for its beautiful lakes, historic surroundings and diverse wildlife. The safari route takes visitors through some of the most scenic landscapes of Ranthambore National Park. Starting near Jogi Mahal, the route follows the edges of Padam Talao, the park's largest lake, before continuing towards Raj Bagh Talao, its historic ruins and the Mandook area. The open lakeshores and surrounding grasslands offer beautiful views across the water, with the historic Ranthambore Fort adding to the scenery.

Visitors may spot sambar deer moving through shallow waters, crocodiles resting along the lake banks, spotted deer, langurs and various bird species in their natural habitat. The combination of lakes, ancient ruins and forest surroundings makes Zone 3 a memorable destination for wildlife enthusiasts and nature photographers.

Famous Tigers of Ranthambore Zone 3

Zone 3 has a long association with some of Ranthambore's well-known tigers and tigresses. The lakeside territory around Padam Talao and Raj Bagh Talao has historically been associated with legendary tigresses such as Machali (T-16), Krishna (T-19) and Arrowhead (T-84). Their presence has contributed to the popularity of this safari area among wildlife enthusiasts.

In recent years, tigress Riddhi (T-124), daughter of Arrowhead, has been associated with the Padam Talao and Raj Bagh landscape. Tiger movements and territorial patterns can change over time as animals naturally move across different forest areas.

Safari reports from October 2026 have also mentioned Riddhi in the Zone 3 area. However, individual tiger sightings, cub movements and territorial activity are subject to change and cannot be guaranteed during future safari drives.

Wildlife Note: Tiger territories and movements are dynamic. Visitors should always follow their safari guide's instructions and maintain a safe distance from wildlife.

Safari Route & Major Attractions

Important areas along the Zone 3 safari route include Jogi Mahal, Padam Talao, Raj Bagh Talao, Mandook and High Point. These locations offer a combination of lakeside scenery, grasslands, rocky surroundings and historic forest landscapes.

Padam Talao and Raj Bagh Talao are particularly attractive for their natural beauty and wildlife habitats. The surrounding ruins, water bodies and views of Ranthambore Fort create excellent opportunities for landscape photography.

Wildlife Photography & Safari Experience

For visitors planning a Ranthambore tiger safari, Zone 3 offers excellent opportunities to enjoy natural landscapes and observe wildlife. A camera with a suitable zoom lens can help capture distant animals and birds without disturbing their natural behaviour. Morning light and changing colours around the lakes can make the safari especially enjoyable for photography enthusiasts.

Every safari offers a different experience depending on weather, season and natural wildlife movement. Although Zone 3 is popular for its scenic lakes and tiger history, sightings of Bengal tigers or any other animal are never guaranteed.

Experience: Scenic lakes, historic ruins, forest landscapes and wildlife photography.

Popular Route Areas: Jogi Mahal, Padam Talao, Raj Bagh Talao, Mandook and High Point.

Zone 4 – Malik Talao, Lakkarda & Tamba Khan

Zone 4 offers an interesting combination of rugged forest terrain, rocky landscapes and natural water points. The safari route passes through Singhdwar, Tamba Khan, Malik Talao, Lakkarda, Berda, Semli and Adidant.

Its varied habitat supports several wildlife species, including Bengal tigers, leopards, deer, wild boars and different bird species. The mix of rocky tracks and forest vegetation gives visitors a different perspective of Ranthambore's wilderness.

Nature lovers can enjoy observing the surrounding forest while looking for animal tracks, bird activity and wildlife movement along the safari route.

Experience: Rocky forest trails, water bodies and natural wildlife habitats.

Popular route areas: Malik Talao, Lakkarda, Berda, Tamba Khan and Adidant.

Zone 5 – Kachida Valley, Dhakra & Bakola

Zone 5 is known for its varied forest surroundings and rocky landscape. The safari route covers Singhdwar, Jokha, Kachida, Dhakra, Bagdah, Bakola and Anatpura, offering visitors an opportunity to explore different natural habitats.

Kachida Valley and the surrounding forest tracks add to the area's scenic appeal. Wildlife enthusiasts may encounter Bengal tigers, leopards, deer, langurs, wild boars and several species of birds during their journey.

The changing terrain and forest surroundings make Zone 5 an interesting option for visitors who want to experience more than just tiger tracking.

Experience: Rocky valleys, forest tracks and varied wildlife habitats.

Popular route areas: Kachida, Jokha, Dhakra, Bagdah and Bakola.

Zone 6 – Kundal, Patwa Baori & Mansarovar

Zone 6, also known as the Kundal safari zone, offers a different forest experience from the central lake areas. Its route includes Rajbag Naka, Palli Darwaja, Kundal, Patwa Baori, Mansarovar and Guda.

The landscape features open forest stretches, rocky surroundings and natural clearings. These habitats support a variety of wildlife, including Bengal tigers, leopards, deer and several bird species. The surrounding scenery also makes this area interesting for wildlife photography.

Zone 6 is worth exploring for visitors interested in experiencing the wider Ranthambore forest landscape beyond the popular lake routes.

Experience: Open forest landscapes, rocky tracks and natural clearings.

Popular route areas: Kundal, Patwa Baori, Mansarovar and Rajbag Naka.

Zone 7 – Chidikho & Jamoda Forest Route

Zone 7, commonly known as Chidikho, is an interesting safari area featuring hilly terrain, forest tracks and natural vegetation. The designated route passes through Rajbag Naka, Chidikho, Jamoda and Kushalidarra.

The area's uneven terrain and forest surroundings create a different wildlife viewing experience. Visitors may come across Bengal tigers, leopards, deer, langurs and a variety of birds depending on animal movement and seasonal conditions.

For nature enthusiasts, this zone offers opportunities to observe the natural environment, explore forest scenery and enjoy a quieter side of Ranthambore's safari landscape.

Experience: Hilly forest trails, natural vegetation and wildlife observation.

Popular route areas: Chidikho, Jamoda and Kushalidarra.

Zone 8 – Balas, Neemli Dang & Mahakho

Zone 8 is also known as the Balas safari zone. Its route passes through forest areas including Nursery, Balas, Neemli Dang, Kalibhat, Kharai and Mahakho. Rocky hills and natural vegetation give this zone a distinctive landscape.

The area offers opportunities to explore different wildlife habitats and observe birds, deer, leopards and other forest animals. Its varied terrain makes it an interesting destination for visitors who enjoy nature photography and birdwatching.

Every safari through Zone 8 offers a chance to appreciate the natural beauty and biodiversity of the wider Ranthambore Tiger Reserve.

Experience: Rocky hills, forest vegetation and birdwatching opportunities.

Popular route areas: Balas, Neemli Dang, Kalibhat, Kharai and Mahakho.

Zone 9 – Qualji, Kamleshwar View Point & Chakal Nadi

Zone 9 covers the Qualji forest landscape and offers visitors a different side of the Ranthambore Tiger Reserve. The route includes Qualji, New Talai, Ghati Ka Tiraha, Kamleshwar Mandir View Point, Chakal Nadi Road, Pandu Kho, Gajipur and Devpura.

Its forest surroundings, hills and natural clearings create opportunities for wildlife observation and nature photography. Visitors may encounter tigers, leopards, deer, wild boars and various bird species during their safari.

Zone 9 is an option for travellers who want to explore a different forest route and experience the wider landscape of Ranthambore beyond its central tourism areas.

Experience: Natural viewpoints, forest roads and diverse wildlife habitats.

Popular route areas: Qualji, New Talai, Kamleshwar View Point and Pandu Kho.

Zone 10 – Aantri, Kailashpuri & Devpura Bandh

Zone 10, also known as the Aantri safari zone, is characterised by its forest routes, rocky surroundings and natural water points. The designated route includes Halonda, Kailashpuri, Aantri, Bebri, Jhojeshwar Mandir View Point and Devpura Bandh.

The combination of forest vegetation, hills and water sources supports a range of wildlife habitats. Visitors may observe Bengal tigers, leopards, hyenas, deer, jackals and different bird species during a safari, depending on natural conditions.

Zone 10 provides another opportunity to discover the varied landscapes of Ranthambore National Park while enjoying a memorable wildlife experience.

Experience: Hilly landscapes, water points and diverse forest wildlife.

Popular route areas: Aantri, Kailashpuri, Bebri and Devpura Bandh.
